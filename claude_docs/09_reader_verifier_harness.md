# 09 · Reader-Verifier Harness (solver calls reader and verifier sub-agents as tools)

- **Name**: the **reader-verifier harness** (owner, 2026-10-28), version 1 of the planned
  reader-verifier-compactor harness. A terminus-2 solver that obtains rule text through a
  **reader** sub-agent and must have its answer file checked by the **verifier** sub-agent
  before `task_complete` is accepted. Both sub-agents are called by the solver through a field
  in its JSON response, run in their own `Chat`, and return a text result into the solver's
  conversation. Version 2 adds a **compactor** sub-agent that rebuilds the solver's context;
  it is implemented since 2026-11-10 (§10). Each sub-agent run has a limit with one fallback
  to no thinking: seconds until 2026-11-10 (§10.9), output tokens since 2026-11-11, when
  time-outs were dropped and inference cost became the second metric (§10.12). Reruns under
  that protocol (§10.12, 2026-11-12): baseline 2/35 at 1.75 cents per task, solver-verifier
  15/35 at 7.07, reader-verifier-compactor 17/35 at 6.51 (the compactor was called once, so
  that run does not test it). First full run `rvc-run1-numeric` (§10.10): 13 of 35
  file-graded, the same as `rv-run1-numeric`, with 14 timeouts instead of 6.
  Error attribution of that run in §10.11: 12 of its 22 failures are tasks the model solves in
  another configuration, 10 are not; the solver omits provisions it never asked the reader
  for, and the verifier named the decisive error in 2 of 18 wrong answers.
- **Date opened**: 2026-10-28
- **Predecessor**: the solver-verifier harness ([08](08_solver_verifier_harness.md)), run-3
  configuration. Its verifier (skill v3, four-step plan, index packet) is reused with a skill
  v4 (four rubrics, §5); its completion protocol is replaced (§3).
- **Code**: `tools/dar_terminus2_multiagent.py` (`Terminus2ReaderVerifier`, a subclass of the
  run-3 verifier agent); one skill per role, `tools/skills/solver_v2/SKILL.md` (v1 was used
  only by `rv-smoke1`), `tools/skills/reader_v1/SKILL.md`, `tools/skills/rubric_verifier_v4/SKILL.md`;
  `tools/templates/terminus-json-multiagent-v2.txt` (the stock terminus-2 template plus a second
  example response that is a sub-agent call and a `{skill}` slot; v1 had the field only as a line
  in the example); `tools/run_dar_qwen35_35b.sh` with `AGENT=t2rv`.
  Version 2: `tools/dar_terminus2_compactor.py` (`Terminus2ReaderVerifierCompactor`),
  `tools/skills/compactor_v1/SKILL.md`, `tools/skills/solver_v3/SKILL.md`,
  `tools/templates/terminus-json-multiagent-v3.txt`, `AGENT=t2rvc`, `tools/replay_compactor.py`.

  [figures/compactor.png](figures/compactor.png) (the solver's context before and after a

## 1. Why: the solver's context is the problem now

Run-3 of the solver-verifier harness ([08](08_solver_verifier_harness.md) §9.2) is the first
terminus configuration that beats the no-verifier baseline on SARA numeric (0.400 vs 0.200,
protocol score), but it is still only at the low edge of the CI of prompt-based direct reasoning
(0.435), which sees the whole 6.1k-token statute in one prompt and nothing else. Three things in
the solver's trajectory account for most of the gap and for the time it costs:

1. **The solver's own search.** A numeric trial spends 6 steps (median) before its first claim
   `grep`-ing and `sed`-ing the statute, each step a thinking-bearing generation at ≈59 ms/token.
   The terminal output of those steps (10,000-byte observations, truncation markers) stays in the
   conversation for the rest of the trial.
2. **Repair turns compound it.** After a verifier report the solver reads more sections and the
   conversation grows again; the three binary damages of run-3 and the right→wrong numeric cases
   all happened in repair turns.
3. **Statute size.** SARA's statute is only 6.1k tokens, so a direct prompt loses nothing by
   including all of it. Any retrieval step can only *remove* information relative to direct; the
   harness has to win on cleaner context, tool-executed arithmetic and verification, not on
   retrieval.

The reader-verifier harness moves the search into a sub-agent whose internal commands and
outputs never enter the solver's conversation. What the solver receives is the **verbatim rule
text** of the ranges the reader selected, extracted from the file by the harness, plus the
reader's one-line relevance notes and a list of what it did not resolve. The verifier is then
called explicitly by the solver instead of being triggered by `task_complete`, and the harness
only checks that the answer file the solver finishes with is the one the verifier last saw.

Design decisions in this document were taken with the owner on 2026-10-28 (decision log in
[04](04_initial_proposal.md) §8). GPT's plan of the same date (two-stage, sub-agents as tools,
no Compactor in version 1, no `state.md`) is the starting point; deviations are marked.

## 2. Components and the call mechanism

| Role | Context | Tools | Called by | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solver** | terminus-2 conversation (stock loop, JSON responses) | tmux shell (unchanged), two sub-agents | Harbor | the answer file |
| **Reader** | fresh `Chat` per call | `environment.exec` in the task container, ≤ `reader_max_steps` command steps (default 3), 6,000 bytes of output per command | solver, any number of times | line ranges + labels + unresolved items; the harness turns the ranges into verbatim text |
| **Verifier** | fresh `Chat` per round | `environment.exec`, ≤ `verifier_max_steps` steps (default 4, as in run-3) | solver, ≤ `max_verify_rounds` times (default 3) | the rubric report, rendered as in run-3 |

**Call mechanism (owner's decision 2026-10-28)**: a top-level field in the solver's JSON
response, at the same level as `task_complete`:

```json
{"analysis": "...", "plan": "...", "commands": [...],
 "subagent": {"name": "reader", "query": "which provisions govern ..."}}

{"analysis": "...", "plan": "...", "commands": [],
 "subagent": {"name": "verifier"}}
```

Harbor's JSON parser ignores unknown top-level fields, so the stock parser, its auto-fixes and
its warnings are untouched; the subclass reads the field from the raw response. In one episode
the commands run first (through tmux, as always), then the sub-agent; the sub-agent's result is
appended to that episode's terminal output as the next message to the solver. A response that
both calls a sub-agent and sets `task_complete` is treated as a call, not as a finish: the
completion flag is ignored and the result says so. Native function calling was not used because
the terminus-2 loop is text-based end to end and the DAR baseline is terminus-2 (plan §2.1 left
this open; decided for the JSON field).

Nothing is intercepted at the shell level. The solver may still read the rule file itself; the
prompt tells it to use the reader, and the harness counts the solver's own reads of the rule file
in `summary.json` (owner's decision: no hard-coded interception).

**Protocol correction (added 2026-11-01 after `rv-smoke1`, §9.1).** The one thing the harness
does correct is a sub-agent call placed where the harness does not read it. A keystroke that is
the call itself (`subagent={...}`, `"subagent": {...}`, `{"name": "reader", ...}`) or a command
object carrying a `subagent` key is **not sent to the shell**; the remaining commands run and a
`=== SUB-AGENT CALL MISPLACED ===` block tells the solver where the field goes and shows the
correct form. If the response does not parse at all and mentions `subagent`, the same form is
appended as a `HINT:` to the stock parse-error feedback. The harness never interprets the
misplaced text as a call (that would be an auto-fix of the protocol; the solver resends it).
These corrections are counted in `summary.json` (`n_protocol_corrections`,
`subagent_call_errors[].kind`) and flagged in the verifier's trajectory index. Unlike a
rule-file read, this is an error in the harness's own protocol and benchmark-independent, which
is why it is handled mechanically while reads are only counted.

## 3. Completion protocol

Stock terminus-2 ends a trial on the second consecutive `task_complete` ("Are you sure?" in
between). The solver-verifier harness kept that and put the verifier in the confirmation
message. Here the verifier is called by the solver, so the protocol changes (owner's decisions
2026-10-28):

| Event | What the harness does |
|---|---|
| Solver calls `verifier` | commands of that episode run; the answer file is read and hashed; verifier round r runs (r ≤ `max_verify_rounds`); the report plus a decision instruction is returned; the hash is recorded as **verified** |
| Solver calls `verifier` with no rounds left | no verifier; the message says so and that `task_complete` will now be accepted as the file stands |
| `task_complete`, hash equals the last verified hash | commands of that episode run first and the file is re-hashed afterwards; if still equal, the **trial ends at once** (no "Are you sure?" step; owner's decision) |
| `task_complete`, hash not verified, rounds remain | not accepted; the message says the current file has not been verified and asks for a verifier call. Counted as an *unverified claim*. After `max_unverified_claims` (default 2) such claims the next one is accepted and the finish is recorded as `unverified_accepted` so the trial cannot loop until the wall clock |
| `task_complete`, hash not verified, no rounds left | accepted; finish recorded as `budget_exhausted` |
| Sub-agent call and `task_complete` in one response | the call runs, `task_complete` is ignored, the result says so |

The instruction to the solver says the rule in words: "call the verifier before finishing; if
you change the answer file after a report, call it again". The harness gate is the mechanical
backstop for a solver that does not follow it (run-3 showed the Qwen verifier ignoring skill-level
budget rules in most later rounds; the solver is not assumed to be more obedient). The gate holds
no checking logic: it compares two hashes and counts rounds. Whether the report was all-`YES` is
irrelevant to it (plan §2.6: verified ≠ correct).

Hashing after the episode's commands closes the "write a new answer in the confirming response"
path that run-1..3 only logged as `unverified_finish`.

## 4. The reader

### 4.1 Inputs (one user message = `SKILL.md` + packet)

```
=== TASK ===
<instruction.md verbatim: facts, question, rule-file path, answer-file path>

=== QUERY (from the solver, call 2 of this task) ===
<the solver's query text>

=== RETURNED EARLIER IN THIS TASK ===
call 1: lines 87-131 (§63(c)(2) standard deduction); lines 412-440 (§3301 rate of tax)

=== BUDGET ===
3 command steps, 6,000 bytes of output per command, 16,000 characters of excerpts in total.
```

The reader sees the task text so that it can locate provisions the facts engage even when the
query does not name them (plan §2.2; owner 2026-10-28: "does not cost many tokens"). The skill
tells it that the **query decides what to return** and the facts serve to resolve the query's
terms and to pick the exceptions that apply; it does not decide how a provision applies. It does
**not** see the answer file or the solver's conversation. The "returned earlier" index is built
by the harness from the reader's own previous results (ranges and labels only); it is a
de-duplication hint, not shared state.

### 4.2 Output and extraction

Every reader reply is one JSON object: `analysis`, `commands`, `result`. `result` is `null`
while it works and, when done,

```json
{"excerpts": [{"lines": "87-131", "label": "§63(c)(2) standard deduction amounts",
               "why": "the taxpayer takes the standard deduction"}],
 "unresolved": ["whether the father is a dependent: §152(d) read, support test not in the facts"]}
```

An excerpt may name a `file`; by default the ranges refer to the rule file (`rule_path`,
default `/app/statute.txt`, DAR's convention in all four subdomains). The harness checks each
range mechanically (two integers, `a ≤ b`, within the file's line count), runs `sed -n 'a,bp'`
on the file in the container and inserts the lines verbatim. Invalid ranges are reported as such
in the result, never guessed or repaired. The total is capped at `reader_max_chars` (default
16,000; the whole SARA statute is ≈24,000 characters): the excerpt that crosses the cap is cut
with a marker giving the lines shown and omitted, and later excerpts are listed by range and
label as not inserted. The model never copies statute text itself; that was ruled a hard
requirement because a 6k-token copy would cost about six minutes of generation.

What the solver receives:

```
=== READER RESULT (call 1 of this task) ===
Query: ...

[1] lines 87-131 of /app/statute.txt · §63(c)(2) standard deduction amounts
    why: the taxpayer takes the standard deduction
<lines 87-131, verbatim>

[2] lines 412-440 of /app/statute.txt · §3301 rate of tax
    why: ...
<verbatim>

Unresolved:
- ...

(2 excerpts, 2,310 characters, nothing truncated; 2 command steps used)
```

A reader call that returns no `result` within its budget (after one closing prompt and one
retry, as for the verifier) produces a result block that says so and lists nothing; the solver
decides what to do next. A reader call with a missing or empty query is answered by the harness
with a one-line error and no sub-agent run.

### 4.3 Skill v1 (`tools/skills/reader_v1/SKILL.md`, 66 lines)

The reader's stated goal is **coverage**, defined in the same words as the verifier's rubric 2: a
provision is covered when its text, with the definitions, conditions, exceptions and
cross-references it depends on, has reached the solver. Then eight one-line ground rules (the
query decides what to return; whole provisions; only lines displayed in this call; say what is
unresolved; never answer; never paraphrase; the file is the only authority; read-only), the
response format and 3-step budget, the inputs, the three steps (map and locate, read, decide
and return, with the `§`-nesting note) and the result schema. Written for rule-grounded tasks in
general; SARA's `§` headings appear only as examples. The reader cannot *check* coverage: it has
neither the answer file nor the trajectory, and it is stateless across calls; that check is the
verifier's (owner, 2026-10-28).

## 5. The verifier (skill v4, `tools/skills/rubric_verifier_v4/SKILL.md`, 101 lines)

(Revised in place 2026-11-01 after `rv-smoke2`, §9.2 finding 1: suggested actions name a
provision for a reader query, never a shell command on the rule file. The smoke1/smoke2 packets
record the text that was in force.)

Mechanics are run-3's: index packet, full trajectory uploaded to the container and read by the
verifier with `grep`/`sed` (owner, 2026-10-28: the trajectory stays available), four-step plan
(locate, read, check, report), `verifier_max_steps=4`, one command step on later rounds, same
renderer. The trajectory index marks each step that called a sub-agent (`reader call N: lines …`,
`verifier round N`).

**Rubrics reorganised from five to four** (owner's decision 2026-10-28, after the question of
which checks belong to which agent). Mechanical checks were dropped rather than moved into the
harness: the answer-file format (three sections, `Answer:` last) is no longer checked anywhere
but stated once in the solver's skill (owner: no hard-coded gate; the solver can do this). The
order follows the proposal's logic ([04](04_initial_proposal.md) §2.1: tool use → coverage →
reasoning), selection before grounding:

| # | Rubric (`name` in the report) | Question | Evidence | `NO` when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tool use (`tool_use`) | Did the tools deliver what the solver treated them as delivering, and was arithmetic done with a tool? | index; reader-result closing lines and "Not inserted" / "Invalid" lists in the trajectory | a reader call with no result, an invalid range or a truncated excerpt treated as delivered; a failed or truncated command treated as complete; arithmetic in prose |
| 2 | Provision coverage (`provision_coverage`) | Did the text the solver relies on, and the text the facts engage, reach the solver? | section map; `grep` of the trajectory for reader ranges and own reads; `sed` of the file | a cited section that does not exist in the file or never reached the solver (cited from memory); a section engaged by an `UNUSED` fact that never reached it |
| 3 | Provision selection and application (`provision_selection`) | Was the governing provision selected and were its conditions and exceptions checked against the facts? | reading the cited and the engaged sections against `## Reasoning` | an engaged provision never considered; a condition treated as met without a named fact; a triggered exception or limitation not applied. `UNUSED`-fact and entailment rules of v3 kept, shortened |
| 4 | Grounded reasoning (`grounded_reasoning`) | Does every number and section reference come from the file or the facts, and do the computations reproduce? | `sed` of the cited sections; `python3` re-run of the solver's stated steps | a section number that does not say what Reasoning uses it for; a number with no source; a computation that does not reproduce |

Relative to v3: rubric 1's format check is gone, its reader part is the new tool-use rubric;
v3's rubric 5 is split, "done with a tool" into rubric 1 and "reproduces" into rubric 4; v3's
rubrics 3 and 4 swap places. A provision counts as having **reached the solver** when its lines
are inside a `READER RESULT` block (the lines actually inserted, not a truncated or "not
inserted" part, nor what the reader read internally) or in the solver's own terminal output;
the solver not having run `grep`/`sed` itself is never a violation. NO vs UNKNOWN, point-do-not-
solve, actionable-only, all-pass ⇒ empty lists are unchanged from v3. The report schema keeps
`unused_facts`, `quoted_lines`, `unresolved` and `suggested_actions`; `RUBRIC_LABELS_V4` in the
agent renders the four names.

## 6. The solver's prompt: stock template + skill

Owner's decision 2026-10-28: the solver's prompt stays as close to stock terminus-2 as possible
and everything specific to this harness goes into a skill, so that all three roles have one.

- `tools/templates/terminus-json-multiagent-v1.txt` (used by `rv-smoke1` only) is the stock
  `terminus-json-plain.txt` with three additions: the `subagent` field in the JSON example, one
  line under "Optional fields" pointing at the skill, and a `Skill:\n{skill}` slot before
  `Task Description:`. The agent fills the slot from the skill file at start-up (braces doubled,
  since the template is `str.format`-ed with the instruction and terminal state later).
- `tools/skills/solver_v1/SKILL.md` (38 lines, `rv-smoke1` only): how to call a sub-agent (one
  per response, commands first); the reader (verbatim text; query as the rule or question, not a
  guessed section number; first call broad, later calls specific; unlimited calls); the verifier
  (four rubrics, `NO` is a checked violation and `UNKNOWN` a lead, never gives the answer, write
  the file first, ≤ 3 rounds); finishing (verifier before finishing, accepted only for the checked
  file, `task_complete` + empty commands ends at once, call + flag = call); the three-section
  answer file (`## Statute` section numbers, `## Reasoning`, `Answer:` last; quoted heredoc)
  and arithmetic in `python3`. The grader is unchanged.
- **v2 of both (2026-11-01, the defaults since then; owner's decision after §9.1).** Diagnosis
  of `rv-smoke1`: both solvers *knew* the reader existed (their first `plan` said "read the
  statute, then use the reader") and both tried to call it at their first opportunity, but put
  the call in the wrong place; the stock preamble ("solve the task by providing batches of shell
  commands") and the task text ("Carefully read the statute at `/app/statute.txt`") outweighed a
  skill sentence in the middle of a 6.9k-character prompt. Changes, prompt-only:
  - `terminus-json-multiagent-v2.txt` shows **two complete example responses**, one with
    commands and one that is a reader call with `"commands": []`; the preamble says the task is
    solved by commands "and, where the skill below says so, by calling a sub-agent"; the
    "Optional fields" line says the field is top-level, never a keystroke, never inside a command
    object. Still the stock template plus one example block and the slot.
  - `solver_v2/SKILL.md` (45 lines) opens with **Rule 1: read the rule document through the
    reader, never yourself**, which addresses the task text head-on ("the task description tells
    you to read the rule document; do that by calling the reader"), names the commands not to use
    (`cat`, `grep`, `sed`, `head`, `less`, `python3` on the file), and prescribes that **the first
    response is a broad reader call** with a given example query. The call mechanics ("top-level
    field next to `commands`") come second; the verifier, finishing and answer-file sections are
    v1's unchanged.
  - Rejected for now: intercepting reads of the rule file (owner, 2026-10-28, stands), and a
    harness-forced first reader call before the solver's first step (two-stage; kept as the
    fallback if v2 does not stop the solver from reading the file itself, because it would make
    the sub-agents something other than tools the solver calls).

## 10. The compactor (version 2: the reader-verifier-compactor harness, 2026-11-10)

Owner's decisions 2026-11-09/10: implement the compactor as a **callable tool** of the solver
(as in SelfCompact, arXiv 2609.37725v1; `paper_review_gpt/11_selfcompact.md`), keep the
verifier's thinking on with no output cap and `verifier_max_steps=3`, and debug harness, skills
and prompts on the tasks `rv-run1-numeric` got wrong, **within one boundary: no answer may leak
and no hint may go beyond the harness level**. Everything below is therefore generic: no skill,
prompt or guard names a task, a fact of a case, a provision of the SARA statute or a value, and
gold labels were never read into a prompt.

Code: `tools/dar_terminus2_compactor.py` (`Terminus2ReaderVerifierCompactor`, a subclass of
`Terminus2ReaderVerifier`; the parent got behaviour-neutral hooks only),
`tools/skills/compactor_v1/SKILL.md`, `tools/skills/solver_v3/SKILL.md`,
`tools/templates/terminus-json-multiagent-v3.txt`, `AGENT=t2rvc` in `tools/run_dar_qwen35_35b.sh`,
`tools/replay_compactor.py` (offline replay, §10.5).

### 10.1 What a compaction does

The compactor **selects, it does not write**. In its own `Chat` (skill + packet) it returns

```json
{"rules": [{"lines": "a-b", "label": "...", "why": "..."}], "steps": [{"step": n, "why": "..."}]}
```

and the harness rebuilds the solver's conversation as

1. the first user message (template + solver skill + task), untouched;
2. the assistant response that led to the compaction;
3. one **state page** as the next user message: state line (rounds used, reader calls,
   compactions), the answer file, the latest verifier report verbatim, the selected rule-file
   lines copied with `sed -n` under `[k] lines a-b of <file> · label`, the terminal output of the
   selected solver steps verbatim, the output of the commands of the current step, and the usual
   decision instruction.

Nothing on the page is model-written except the one-line `label` and `why` of each range. The
removed messages are saved in the container (`/tmp/compactor/history-N.txt`, named on the page)
and locally (`call-N.context-before.json`). Files in the container, verifier rounds and reader
counts are untouched. The verifier is told through its trajectory index that from a compaction
on the solver saw only the task and the page; after a compaction the reader's packet lists
"IN THE SOLVER'S CONTEXT NOW" instead of everything returned earlier.

### 10.2 When it runs

| Trigger | Condition | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Solver call | `"subagent": {"name": "compactor"}`; refused below `compactor_min_tokens` | 8,000 |
| After a report | report has a NO and rounds remain: the decision instruction recommends the call (`compact_after_report=hint`), or the harness runs it at once (`auto`), or neither (`off`) | `hint` |
| Context note | one-time `=== CONTEXT NOTE ===` above `compactor_hint_tokens` | 16,000 |
| Forced | after a plain command step above `compactor_force_tokens` | 30,000 |

At most `max_compactions=3` per task. Sizes are the prompt tokens of the solver's latest call.

### 10.3 Packet, selection rules and guards

The packet holds the task, the answer file, the latest report, the rule ranges now in the
solver's context, an **outline of the rule file** (line number and heading of every line that
matches `outline_pattern`, by default a section `§…` or a top-level subsection `(x) …` in
column 0; 37 lines for the SARA statute), the solver's history since its context starts (rule
text replaced by placeholders, long output clipped; the full text is in a file) and the budget.

Harness-side rules, all deterministic:

- **Outline snapping.** Every selected range is widened to outline boundaries (back to the
  outline line at or before its start, forward to the line before the next outline line), so a
  provision is never cut and the compactor does not need to display text before naming it.
- **Coverage guard.** If the answer file contains the token of a section heading (the first
  word of an outline line matching `section_pattern`, e.g. `§63`) and the selection has no line
  from that section, the ranges of that section that the solver has in context are appended,
  labelled "restored by the harness". Nothing is added for a section the solver never received.
- **Budget** `compactor_max_chars=14000` of rule text (ranges beyond it are listed by line
  range only), `compactor_keep_steps=4` steps of up to 2,500 characters each; steps that only
  write or show the answer file are not eligible.
- **Abort.** No result, or no valid rule text while the context holds rule text: no rebuild,
  the solver continues with its context unchanged.
- **Time.** `compactor_timeout_sec=180` per attempt; after a timeout one more attempt without
  thinking (`chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking=false` for that call only,
  `compactor_fallback=true`); `compactor_thinking=false` skips the first attempt.
- Free-text notes exist behind `compactor_notes_chars` (0 = off, the default).

Skill v1 (`compactor_v1/SKILL.md`): select only; everything not named is removed; ranges follow
the outline; subsections rather than whole sections; keep what a provision depends on; what the
report names must be on the page; one pass; first the provisions of report items that are not a
YES, then the rest the answer file cites; one step for the computation behind the figures.

Solver skill v3 = v2 plus the compactor's description, "when to call the compactor" (after a
report with a NO before working on it; when told the context has grown; never before the answer
file exists) and "acting on a verifier report" (a report item is a claim to check against rule
text before the file is changed).

### 10.9 Time limits for the reader and the verifier (2026-11-10)

> Superseded on 2026-11-11 (§10.12): the limits now count output tokens, not seconds. The
> mechanism below is unchanged; the wall-clock settings still exist and default to none.

Owner's decision on open item (a) of §10.8: the reader and the verifier get the compactor's
time control, a wall-clock limit and one retry without thinking. Thinking stays on by
default; the limit only bounds what one sub-agent run can take from the trial's 1,800 s.

**Mechanism** (`SubagentClock` in `tools/dar_terminus2_verifier.py`, used by the verifier
round and, through `_run_tool_subagent`, by the reader):

1. One sub-agent run (all its model calls and command steps) gets `*_timeout_sec` seconds.
2. A model call still in flight at the limit is cancelled. vLLM aborts the request when the
   client disconnects (✅ `rvc-timeout-forced`: never more than two running requests with
   two concurrent trials).
3. That call is sent again with `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking=false`, and the rest of
   the run continues without thinking under a fresh limit of the same length.
4. If the second limit passes too, the run ends without a result.

One difference from the compactor: the compactor restarts its run from an empty context
(it has one command step, so little is lost); the reader and the verifier have up to three
command steps, so the fallback **continues in the same context** and keeps the steps already
completed. Only the cancelled call is repeated.

| Setting (`--ak`) | Default in `t2rv` / `t2rvc` | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `reader_timeout_sec` | 180 | limit of one reader call; `none` = no limit |
| `reader_thinking` | true | false = the reader never thinks |
| `reader_fallback` | true | false = no second phase, the run ends at the limit |
| `verifier_timeout_sec` | 300 | limit of one verifier round (`t2verify`: none) |
| `verifier_thinking` | true | |
| `verifier_fallback` | true | |

**Why 180 s and 300 s** (✅ durations from `rvc-smoke1` and `rvc-smoke2-auto`, no limit):
32 of 36 reader calls took 38–147 s and four took 290–698 s, so 180 s, the compactor's
value, cuts only the outliers. The 13 verifier rounds took 140–836 s (median 438 s), and the
first model call of a round alone took 146–269 s in 11 of 13 rounds. A limit of 180 s on the
round would end the thinking phase inside the first call nearly every time, which amounts
to a verifier without thinking and contradicts the decision to keep it on. With 300 s a
round thinks for up to five minutes and then finishes without thinking; the worst case is
600 s on paper and about 400 s in practice (a round without thinking needs four short
calls). 🔶 The value is a first setting, not tuned.

**When a run ends without a result**:

- Reader: the solver gets `No result: the reader did not finish within its time limit.
  Nothing was inserted. You may call the reader again, with a narrower query.`
- Verifier: the report reads `No report: the verifier did not finish within its time limit.`
  The round is used up and the answer file does **not** count as verified. The same now holds
  for a round that ends without a report for any other reason (step budget, context limit);
  until 2026-11-10 such a round marked the answer file as verified although nothing had been
  checked, the same gap as the one closed for failed rounds on 2026-11-02 (§9.3).

**Logs**: `reader/call-N.json` and `rubric_verifier/round-N.json` gain `attempts` (one entry
per phase: thinking, outcome `finished` or `timeout`, seconds, model calls completed),
`thinking_fallback` and `timed_out`; `summary.json` gains the six settings and
`reader_thinking_fallbacks`, `reader_timeouts`, `verifier_thinking_fallbacks`,
`verifier_timeouts`. The tokens of a cancelled call are not counted anywhere, because the
request is aborted before the server reports usage; token totals of a run with fallbacks are
therefore a lower bound.

**Check with forced limits** (✅ 2026-11-10, `rvc-timeout-forced`, tax-case 78 and 97 with
`reader_timeout_sec=20 verifier_timeout_sec=45`, stopped by hand once both paths had run):
15 reader calls and 2 verifier rounds each logged a thinking phase cut at the limit and a
second phase without thinking cut at the limit again (a run without thinking needs more than
20 s for three steps, as intended for this test); the solver received the two messages above,
neither round recorded a verified hash, and the trials went on.

- (e) Put the whole rule file, or at least its outline with one line per section, in front of

- (f) Make completeness the verifier's main question: for every stated fact, name the provision
  that uses it or the one that rules it out; for every section the answer applies, read the
  whole section and list the clauses whose conditions the facts meet and the answer does not
  mention. Move the two process rubrics out of the model: the harness can check tool use and
  delivered line ranges from the trajectory itself.
- (g) Gate on the verdict, not on the hash alone, and reject a report with missing rubrics.

### 10.12 No time-outs: inference cost as the second metric, token limits (2026-11-11)

Owner's decision (`04` §8): wall-clock time is no longer a limit and not a metric. It
depends on the machine, the vLLM settings and the number of concurrent trials; in
`rvc-run1-numeric` the 1,800 s limit ended 14 of 35 trials at two concurrent trials (§10.10).
The second metric next to accuracy is **inference cost**: the tokens a trial consumed, priced
with a fixed card. It is not the break-even price of `04` §3.3, which prices wrong answers
and human review; inference cost prices the model calls only.

**Price card** (`tools/pricing/price_card.json`, ✅ fetched 2026-11-11 from OpenRouter, model
`qwen/qwen3.5-35b-a3b`; the two API responses are stored next to it):

| Token kind | USD per million |
|---|---|
| input | 0.14 |
| output (thinking included) | 1.00 |
| cache read | 0.05 |

The card has no cache-write fee. It is fixed: later price changes on OpenRouter do not
change our numbers. One output token costs as much as seven input tokens.

**Two costs per trial**, both from `tools/inference_cost.py`:

- *No cache*: input tokens x 0.14 + output tokens x 1.00. An upper bound. The cost budget
  below is counted at this price.
- *With cache*: a prompt cache simulated offline inside the trial. The part of a prompt that
  is a prefix of an earlier prompt of the same trial is billed at the cache-read price, the
  rest at the input price. The earlier prompt can belong to another role: the second reader
  call shares the skill text with the first. Generated tokens are not counted as cached:
  thinking is on and the reasoning is not sent back, so the next prompt leaves the server's
  token sequence where the previous prompt ended. No minimum prefix length and no expiry are
  modelled, so this is a lower bound for a provider with prefix caching.
- Nothing is cached across tasks. Every task is priced as if it ran alone, so the result
  does not depend on the order or the number of concurrent trials.
- 🔶 Approximation: prefixes are measured in characters. When the shared prefix is a whole
  earlier prompt its token count is used (exact up to the few tokens of the chat template's
  generation prompt); otherwise tokens are taken in proportion to characters.

**Call ledger** (`tools/dar_cost_ledger.py`, `CostLedgerMixin`, a base class of `t2fix`,
`t2verify*`, `t2rv` and `t2rvc`): every model call of a trial is appended to
`agent/llm_calls.jsonl` when it returns or fails.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `n`, `role`, `started`, `seconds` | call number in the trial; `solver`, `reader`, `verifier`, `compactor` or `summarizer` |
| `thinking`, `max_tokens` | as sent |
| `prompt_tokens`, `completion_tokens` | usage as the server reported it (`n_replies` > 1: the LLM layer retried, sums) |
| `finish` | `stop`, `length` (cut off by `max_tokens`), `context`, `cancelled`, `error` |
| `prompt_chars`, `lcp_chars`, `lcp_call` | length of the prompt and the longest prefix it shares with an earlier prompt, with that call's number |
| `spent_usd` | running no-cache cost of the trial |

A reply cut off by an output limit is in the ledger with its tokens. Harbor's own counters
drop such a reply, so at the end of the trial `n_input_tokens`, `n_output_tokens` and
`cost_usd` of `result.json` are overwritten from the ledger (`cost_usd` = no-cache cost).
Runs made before the ledger are priced from the solver's `episode-N/debug.json` (exact
prompts and usage) and the sub-agent trajectories; requests that failed or were cancelled
left no usage there and are missing from their cost.

**What stops a run now**

| Limit | Value | At the limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cost budget per task, `cost_budget_usd` (`COST_BUDGET_USD`) | 0.15 USD, no-cache price | checked before each solver step; the step in progress (with its sub-agent runs) is finished, then the trial ends and the answer file is graded as it is. `agent/cost_budget.json` records budget, amount spent and `budget_exhausted` |
| Output tokens of one model call, `max_tokens` (`MAX_TOKENS`) | 16,384 | the reply is cut; terminus-2 handles it as before |
| Output tokens of one reader call, `reader_max_output_tokens` | 8,000 | the model call that reached the limit is sent again without thinking and the run continues without thinking under a fresh limit; reached again: no result |
| Output tokens of one verifier round, `verifier_max_output_tokens` | 12,000 | same; reached again: no report, the round is used up |
| Output tokens of one compactor call, `compactor_max_output_tokens` | 4,000 | the call is repeated from an empty context without thinking; reached again: the context stays as it is |
| Request timeout (`REQUEST_TIMEOUT`), agent timeout (`AGENT_TIMEOUT_MULT`) | 7,200 s; 600 s x 24 | safety nets against a hung server. A trial that reaches one is an infrastructure failure and is run again, not scored |

- The token limits count completion tokens, thinking included, summed over the model calls
  of one sub-agent run. Each call is sent with `max_tokens` = what is left of the limit, so
  the limit cannot be passed. The mechanism is the `SubagentClock` of §10.9 with tokens in
  place of seconds; `attempts` entries now carry `completion_tokens` and the outcome
  `output_limit`, and the call records gain `limit_reached`.
- The wall-clock settings of §10.9 (`reader_timeout_sec`, `verifier_timeout_sec`,
  `compactor_timeout_sec`) still exist and default to none. `rvc-run1-numeric` is reproduced
  with 180 / 300 / 180, `--ak reader_max_output_tokens=none --ak
  verifier_max_output_tokens=none --ak compactor_max_output_tokens=none`,
  `COST_BUDGET_USD=none AGENT_TIMEOUT_MULT=3 REQUEST_TIMEOUT=600`.
- Waits inside the container (command durations, `exec` time-outs) are unchanged. They
  bound shell commands, not the model, and do not depend on the server's speed.
- With time out of the picture, `N_CONCURRENT` only changes how long a job takes.

**Why these values** (✅ measured on the existing runs; chosen by Claude under the owner's
delegation, 🔶 first settings, not tuned):

- *0.15 USD.* No-cache cost per trial on SARA numeric: baseline mean 1.6 cents (max 3.7),
  solver-verifier mean 6.5 (95th percentile 11.2, max 11.7), reader-verifier-compactor mean
  5.0 (max 8.4, cut short by time-outs). 15 cents is above every trial on record and about
  three times the mean of our harnesses: it ends runaway trials and leaves normal ones alone.
- *Reader 8,000, verifier 12,000.* Output tokens per run in `rv-run1-numeric`, which had no
  limits: reader median 2.6k, 90th percentile 7.0k, 95th 9.7k, max 13.8k; verifier median
  7.3k, 90th 11.2k, 95th 12.0k, max 13.4k. The limits sit near the 95th percentile, so about
  one run in twenty falls back to no thinking.
- *Compactor 4,000.* 95th percentile 2.7k, max 3.2k in `rvc-run1-numeric`.
- For comparison, the time limits of §10.9 fired far more often in `rvc-run1-numeric`: the
  reader fell back in 23 of 92 calls, the verifier in 34 of 50 rounds, the compactor in 4 of
  22 calls. At about 25 output tokens per second, 300 s is 7,500 tokens, the verifier's
  median need.

**Finding: there was a hidden time limit per model call** (✅ 2026-11-11). litellm's default
request timeout is 600 s and Harbor does not change it. The longest solver replies on record
end just below it (573 to 596 s, the largest 15,262 tokens in 595.7 s), and 8 solver requests
of the four numeric runs have no reply in their `debug.json`. A request that timed out was
retried up to three times by the LLM layer and its tokens were never counted. The 16,384-token
limit per turn (decision of 2026-10-15) was applied only as the server's default; at 17 to 25
tokens per second the 600 s bound came first, at 10k to 15k tokens. Both are now explicit:
`max_tokens=16384` is sent with every request and the request timeout is 7,200 s.
