## Statute

From §3306(a)(1)(B) (line 261):

"(B) on each of some 10 days during the calendar year or during the preceding calendar year, each day being in a different calendar week, employed at least one individual in employment for some portion of the day."

## Reasoning

Section 3306(a)(1)(B) defines an "employer" as a person who, "on each of some 10 days during the calendar year... each day being in a different calendar week, employed at least one individual in employment for some portion of the day."

Alice employed Bob on the following dates in 2017:
- Jan 24, Feb 4, Mar 3, Mar 18, Apr 1, May 9, Oct 14, Oct 25, Nov 8, Nov 22, Dec 1, Dec 2

Using Python to calculate ISO week numbers:

```python
from datetime import date
dates = [
    date(2017, 1, 24),  # Week 4
    date(2017, 2, 4),   # Week 5
    date(2017, 3, 3),   # Week 9
    date(2017, 3, 18),  # Week 11
    date(2017, 4, 1),   # Week 13
    date(2017, 5, 9),   # Week 19
    date(2017, 10, 14), # Week 41
    date(2017, 10, 25), # Week 43
    date(2017, 11, 8),  # Week 45
    date(2017, 11, 22), # Week 47
    date(2017, 12, 1),  # Week 48
    date(2017, 12, 2)   # Week 48
]
unique_weeks = set(d.isocalendar()[1] for d in dates)
# Result: 11 unique weeks (4, 5, 9, 11, 13, 19, 41, 43, 45, 47, 48)
```

Computation: len(unique_weeks) = 11

The statute requires 10 days in 10 different calendar weeks. Alice employed Bob on 11 different calendar weeks, which exceeds the 10-week threshold. Therefore, Section 3306(a)(1)(B) applies to Alice for the year 2017.
