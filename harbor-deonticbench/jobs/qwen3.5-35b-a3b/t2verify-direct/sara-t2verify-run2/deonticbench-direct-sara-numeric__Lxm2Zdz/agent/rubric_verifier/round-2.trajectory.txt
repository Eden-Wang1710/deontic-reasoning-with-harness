**Step 1: Determine filing status**
Alice files a joint return with her spouse. Per §1(a), the tax brackets for "Married individuals filing joint returns" apply.

**Step 2: Calculate income tax**

**Step 2a: Standard deduction**
Per §152(c)(2)(A), the basic standard deduction for a joint return is 200% of the amount in §152(c)(2)(C), which is $3,000.
Standard deduction = 200% × $3,000 = $6,000

**Step 2b: Exemption phaseout**
Per §151(d)(1), the exemption amount is $2,000 per exemption.
Alice and spouse each get one exemption: 2 × $2,000 = $4,000

Per §151(d)(3)(A) and §68(b)(A), the applicable amount for joint returns is $300,000.
Alice's gross income is $684,642.

Excess AGI = $684,642 - $300,000 = $384,642

Per §151(d)(3)(B), the applicable percentage is 2 percentage points for each $2,500 (or fraction thereof).
Number of $2,500 fractions = ceiling($384,642 / $2,500) = ceiling(153.8568) = 154
Applicable percentage = 154 × 2% = 308%

Per §151(d)(3)(B), the applicable percentage cannot exceed 100%.
Therefore, exemption amount = $4,000 × 100% = $0 (fully phased out)

**Step 2c: Taxable income**
Taxable income = Gross income - Standard deduction - Exemptions
Taxable income = $684,642 - $6,000 - $0 = $678,642

**Step 2d: Tax calculation**
Per §1(a)(v), since taxable income ($678,642) is over $250,000:
Income tax = $75,528.50 + 39.6% × ($678,642 - $250,000)
Income tax = $75,528.50 + 0.396 × $428,642
Income tax = $75,528.50 + $169,742.23
Income tax = $245,270.73

**Step 3: Calculate employment tax for agricultural labor**

**Step 3a: Determine if Alice is an employer**
Per §3306(a)(2)(B), an agricultural labor employer is one who "on each of some 10 days during the calendar year...employed at least 5 individuals in employment in agricultural labor."

- Mar 19: 5 workers
- Apr 2: 5 workers
- May 9: 5 workers
- Oct 15: 5 workers

Alice employed 5 workers on 10 different days, meeting the threshold in §3306(a)(2)(B).

**Step 3b: Calculate total wages**

Worker counts from payment schedule:
- Bob: 10 occasions
- Cameron: 11 occasions
- Dan: 9 occasions
- Emily: 10 occasions
- Fred: 9 occasions
- George: 8 occasions

Total occasions = 10 + 11 + 9 + 10 + 9 + 8 = 57

**Step 3c: Calculate taxable wages**

Since no worker earned more than $7,000 (max was Cameron with 11 × $632 = $6,952), all wages are taxable.
Taxable wages = $36,024

**Step 3d: Employment tax calculation**

Employment tax = 6% × $36,024 = $2,161.44

Total tax = Income tax + Employment tax
