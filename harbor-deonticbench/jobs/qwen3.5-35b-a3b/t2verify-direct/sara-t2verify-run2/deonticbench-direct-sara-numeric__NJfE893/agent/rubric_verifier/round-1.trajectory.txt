## Reasoning

**Step 1: Determine Bob's filing status for 2018**

Alice died on July 9, 2014. For the 2018 tax year, Alice's death was 4 years prior, which exceeds the "two years immediately preceding the taxable year" requirement in section 2(a)(1)(A). Therefore, Bob does not qualify as a surviving spouse.

Bob is unmarried at the close of 2018 (spouse died in 2014).

**Step 2: Determine if Bob qualifies as head of household**

Bob furnished the costs of maintaining the home where he and Charlie lived during 2015-2019. For 2018, Bob furnished over half the cost of maintaining the household.

Charlie has no income and is not the qualifying child of any taxpayer. Under section 152(d), Charlie qualifies as a qualifying relative because:
- Charlie has no income (section 152(d)(1)(B))
- Charlie is not a qualifying child of any taxpayer (given in facts)
- Charlie has the same principal place of abode as Bob and is a member of Bob's household (section 152(d)(2)(H))

Therefore, Charlie is a dependent of Bob under section 151(c).

Under section 2(b)(1)(A)(ii), Bob qualifies as head of household because he maintains a household that is the principal place of abode of Charlie, who is a dependent of Bob.

**Step 3: Calculate taxable income**

Bob's AGI: $304,598 (given in facts)

Standard deduction for head of household in 2018: $12,000 (section 63(c)(7)(i))

Exemption amount for 2018: $0 (section 151(d)(5))

Taxable income = AGI - Standard deduction = $304,598 - $12,000 = $292,598

**Step 4: Calculate tax**

Taxable income of $292,598 is over $250,000, so under section 1(b)(v):
Tax = $77,485 + 39.6% × ($292,598 - $250,000)
