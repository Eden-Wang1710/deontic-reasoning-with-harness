## Reasoning
Alice's spouse Harold died on Feb 28, 2016, which is within the two years immediately preceding the 2017 taxable year. Alice maintains a household with her son who is a dependent (qualifying child under §152(c)). The son was born Jan 25, 2000, making him 17 years old at the end of 2017 (less than 25), has the same principal place of abode as Alice for more than half the year, and has not filed a joint return. Therefore, Alice qualifies as a surviving spouse under §2(a) and is taxed under §1(a)(2).

The statute does not contain any provision for business expense deductions. Therefore, Alice's AGI equals her gross income of $236,422.

Alice qualifies for the standard deduction of $6,000 (200% of $3,000 for surviving spouse under §63(c)(2)(A)) and personal exemptions of $4,000 ($2,000 for herself + $2,000 for her dependent son under §151(b)(1) and §151(c)). Under §68(b)(1)(A), the phaseout threshold for surviving spouse is $300,000. Since Alice's AGI of $236,422 is below this threshold, no phaseout applies.

Taxable income = $236,422 - $6,000 - $4,000 = $226,422.

Using §1(a)(2) tax brackets for surviving spouses: Taxable income of $226,422 falls in bracket (iv) (over $140,000 but not over $250,000).
Tax = $35,928.50 + 36% × ($226,422 - $140,000) = $35,928.50 + $31,111.92 = $67,040.42, rounded to $67,040.
