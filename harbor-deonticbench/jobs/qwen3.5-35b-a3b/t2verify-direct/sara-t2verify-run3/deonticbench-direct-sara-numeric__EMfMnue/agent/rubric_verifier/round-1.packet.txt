## Reasoning
Alice's spouse Harold died on Feb 28, 2016, which is within the two years immediately preceding the 2017 taxable year. Alice maintains a household with her son who is a dependent (qualifying child under §152(c)). Therefore, Alice qualifies as a surviving spouse under §2(a) and is taxed under §1(a)(2).

Alice's gross income of $236,422 minus business expenses for agricultural labor ($30,072 = 6 workers × $5,012) gives AGI of $206,350. Alice paid over $20,000 for agricultural labor, making her an employer under §3306(a)(2), so the wages are deductible business expenses.

Alice qualifies for the standard deduction of $6,000 (200% of $3,000 for surviving spouse under §151(c)(2)(A)) and personal exemptions of $4,000 ($2,000 for herself + $2,000 for her dependent son under §151(b)(1) and §151(c)). No phaseout applies since AGI $206,350 is below the $300,000 threshold.

Taxable income = $206,350 - $6,000 - $4,000 = $196,350.

Using §1(a)(2) tax brackets for surviving spouses: Taxable income of $196,350 falls in bracket (iv) (over $140,000 but not over $250,000).
Tax = $35,928.50 + 36% × ($196,350 - $140,000) = $35,928.50 + $20,106 = $56,034.50, rounded to $56,035.
